### Relationship Scheduling for Polyamorous Families

**License:** Open Source (license TBD)

## 1. Product Vision

Pod Life is a scheduling and relationship-time management tool designed specifically for polyamorous people and their families. It solves the single most common logistical pain point in polyamorous life: the overwhelming complexity of coordinating quality time across multiple loving relationships, each with their own needs, rhythms, and constraints.

The product philosophy is warmth-first. Pod Life should feel like a caring friend who helps you show up for the people you love — not a corporate resource allocation tool. Every design decision should ask: *does this make someone feel closer to their partners, or more like a calendar entry?*

### Core Promise

You tell Pod Life who you love, how much time you need and want with each of them, and when you're free. Pod Life proposes a schedule that gets everyone as close to their ideal as possible — fairly, transparently, and with minimal friction.

### Target User

Polyamorous individuals and families in any configuration: triads, quads, polycules, V-structures, solo-poly with multiple partners, relationship anarchists with fluid boundaries. The system must be configuration-agnostic — it does not impose a model of what polyamory "should" look like.

### Open Source Strategy

Pod Life is designed as an open-source project with a reference hosted deployment that may operate on a freemium or low-cost subscription basis. The architecture should be self-hostable for privacy-conscious users and communities.

## 2. Core Concepts & Data Model

### 2.1 Entities

**Person**
The atomic unit. A Person has an account, one or more connected calendars, and relationships to other People. A Person exists independent of any Pod.

- Display name (chosen, not legal)
- Auth identity (magic link email + OAuth tokens for calendars)
- Timezone
- Connected calendars (Google, iCloud, Outlook)
- Global solo/rest time constraints
- Telegram handle (optional)

**Partner Relationship**
A bidirectional link between two People who are in a romantic/intimate relationship. Both people must accept the link. A Partner Relationship carries scheduling preferences.

- Person A ↔ Person B
- Status: invited / active / paused / archived
- Scheduling preferences (per person, per direction):
  - **Needs** (hard minimums): "I need at least X hours/week with this person"
  - **Preferences** (soft targets): "I'd like Y hours/week with this person"
  - Event type breakdown (see below)

**Pod**
A named group of People who are in some configuration of relationship with each other. A Pod is a visibility and coordination boundary. Not everyone in a Pod must be partnered with everyone else — a V-structure has three people in a Pod where two of them are metamours, not partners.

- Pod name (user-chosen, e.g., "The Nest," "Constellation")
- Members (list of People)
- Pod-level scheduling preferences:
  - Full-pod gathering frequency and duration targets
  - Sub-group configuration preferences (e.g., "A+B+C want brunch monthly")
- Scheduling cadence: weekly / biweekly / monthly
- Planning horizon: how far ahead to schedule (1–8 weeks)
- Proposal review window: hours before proposals auto-lock

**Event Type**
A tagged category of quality time, each with a default duration estimate that can be overridden per-person or per-relationship.

| Type | Default Duration | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| Date Night | 3–4 hours | Evening out or intentional 1:1 time |
| Overnight | 12 hours | Evening through morning, blocks next AM |
| Daytime Hang | 2–3 hours | Casual daytime quality time |
| Pod Gathering | 3–4 hours | Full pod together |
| Sub-group Hang | 2–3 hours | Named subset of a pod |
| Custom | User-defined | User-created types with custom labels |

**Time Block**
A proposed or confirmed calendar event generated by the optimizer.

- Type (event type tag)
- Participants (which People)
- Start/end time
- Status: proposed → accepted → locked / declined / reshuffled
- Source pod (which Pod's cycle generated this)
- Satisfaction contribution (how much of someone's need/preference this fulfills)

### 2.2 Relationship Graph

The data model is a **labeled graph**:

- **Nodes** = People
- **Edges** = Partner Relationships (bidirectional, carry per-direction preferences)
- **Hyperedges** = Pods (groups of nodes with shared scheduling context)

A Person can be in multiple Pods. A Partner Relationship can exist independent of any Pod (two people dating who haven't formed a pod yet) or within one or more Pods.

Critical invariant: **a Person's preferences are global across all their relationships**, not scoped to a single Pod. The optimizer sees the full graph for each Person, even though visibility is scoped to Pods.

## 3. User Flows

### 3.1 Onboarding

```
Create account (email magic link)
  → Set display name, timezone
  → Connect calendar(s) via OAuth (free/busy only)
  → Optional: connect Telegram

First relationship:
  → "Add a partner" → generates invite link (bearer token, shared via any channel)
  → Partner clicks link → public preview shows who invited them
  → Partner signs in (or creates an account with any email) → accepts → both set preferences
  → Optional: "Create a pod" → name it, invite members (same unified invite flow,
    `kind: 'pod'`; pods are horizontal so any member can later invite more people)
```

**Invite flow design (locked in):** invitations are bearer-token links — no email gating. Anyone with the link can accept, including users who don't yet have an account (signup-on-accept piggy-backs on the standard login-code flow with `?next=/join/:token`). Partnership and pod membership are orthogonal: joining a pod does *not* implicitly partner you with the other pod members, supporting metamour relationships where members share a pod without all being romantically connected.

### 3.2 Preference Setting

Each person sets preferences **per partner relationship**:

```
For [Partner Name]:
  ├── Needs (hard minimums)
  │   ├── Minimum hours per [week/month]: ___
  │   └── Minimum date nights per [week/month]: ___
  │
  ├── Preferences (soft targets)
  │   ├── Ideal hours per [week/month]: ___
  │   └── Desired breakdown:
  │       ├── Date nights: ___
  │       ├── Overnights: ___
  │       ├── Daytime hangs: ___
  │       └── Custom types: ___
  │
  └── Constraints
      ├── Preferred days/times: ___
      ├── Blocked days/times: ___
      └── Recurring holds: "Tuesdays are always for [Partner]"
```

Global personal settings:

```
Solo / Rest Time:
  ├── Minimum unscheduled evenings per week: ___
  ├── Minimum unscheduled weekend days per month: ___
  └── Blocked personal time windows: ___
```

### 3.3 The Scheduling Cycle

```
1. COLLECT (automated)
   │  Pull free/busy from all connected calendars
   │  Aggregate across all members of all relevant pods
   │  Identify candidate time windows per pair/group
   │
2. OPTIMIZE (automated)
   │  Run global person-centric solver (see §5)
   │  Generate proposed schedule
   │  Calculate satisfaction scores per person
   │
3. PROPOSE (human review)
   │  Push proposed schedule to all affected people
   │  Show satisfaction scores: "You're getting 85% of your
   │  ideal time with Alex, 70% with Jordan"
   │  Show gaps: "Could not find time for a pod gathering
   │  this week — everyone's Saturdays conflict"
   │  Notify via in-app + Telegram
   │
4. NEGOTIATE (human, time-boxed)
   │  People can:
   │  ├── Accept the proposal as-is
   │  ├── Suggest swaps ("Can Tuesday move to Wednesday?")
   │  ├── Flag conflicts ("I forgot I have a work thing Thursday")
   │  └── Adjust preferences for next cycle
   │  System re-runs solver on each change request
   │  Review window = configurable (default 48 hours)
   │
5. LOCK (automated)
   │  After review window closes (or everyone accepts)
   │  Time blocks written to calendars as events
   │  Status moves to "locked"
   │
6. RESHUFFLE (on-demand, mid-cycle)
      Someone needs to change a locked block
      ├── System treats all OTHER locked blocks as hard constraints
      ├── Re-optimizes only the affected person's schedule
      ├── Proposes swap to affected partner(s)
      └── Swap requires acceptance from other participant(s)
```

### 3.4 Telegram Integration

**Pod Group Thread:**
- Bot posts proposed schedule when cycle runs
- Bot posts when proposals are locked
- Members can react/discuss in natural conversation
- Bot summarizes changes if reshuffles happen

**Individual DM Bot:**
- Preference setting via conversational interface ("How much time do you want with Alex this month?")
- Notifications: proposals ready, swap requests, satisfaction summaries
- Quick actions: "Accept this week's schedule" / "I need to reshuffle Thursday"
- Privacy-safe: never reveals other pods' details or partners outside shared pods

## 4. Privacy & Security Model

This is the most sensitive aspect of the product. Pod Life handles information about people's intimate relationships. The privacy model must be airtight.

### 4.1 Core Privacy Principles

1. **You only see people and schedules within pods you belong to.** If Person A is in Pod 1 (with B and C) and Pod 2 (with D and E), then B and C cannot see D and E, and vice versa — unless they share a pod.

2. **Free/busy is opaque.** When the optimizer says "this time isn't available," it never reveals *why*. To Pod 1, Tuesday evening is simply "unavailable" — it doesn't say "because A is on a date night with someone from Pod 2."

3. **Preferences are private by default.** Person A's stated needs/preferences for Partner B are visible only to A (and optionally shared with B if A chooses). Pod members don't see each other's numeric preference settings unless explicitly shared.

4. **Calendar data is minimized.** Pod Life only accesses free/busy status, never event titles, descriptions, attendees, or other metadata. Calendar OAuth scopes should request the minimum possible permission.

5. **Relationship existence is confidential.** The system never reveals to any user that another user has partners or pods they're not part of. Even aggregate stats ("you have 3 pods") are only visible to the account holder.

### 4.2 Technical Privacy Controls

- All calendar data is processed ephemerally during optimization runs and not stored persistently beyond the current cycle's candidate windows
- Encrypted at rest for all relationship and preference data (AES-256)
- No analytics or tracking beyond operational metrics
- Self-hostable deployment for maximum data sovereignty
- Account deletion removes all data, including from other users' views (partner relationships become "deleted user")

### 4.3 Threat Model Considerations

- **Timing inference attack:** If Partner B knows A is always unavailable Tuesday nights and that A has other partners, B can infer A has a recurring date. Mitigation: the system could optionally add jitter or suggest varied scheduling to reduce pattern inference. This is an opt-in "privacy mode."
- **Metadata leakage via Telegram:** Bot messages must never include partner names or pod names from other contexts. All Telegram messages are scoped to the specific pod thread or individual DM.
- **Shared device risk:** PWA should support session timeout, biometric lock (on supported devices), and PIN protection.

## 5. Optimization Engine Design

### 5.1 Problem Formulation

The scheduling problem is a **multi-agent weighted interval scheduling problem with soft and hard constraints**. Formally:

**Given:**
- A set of People P = {p₁, p₂, ..., pₙ}
- A set of Partner Relationships R ⊆ P × P
- For each relationship (pᵢ, pⱼ) ∈ R: needs (hard minimums) and preferences (soft targets) for time together, broken down by event type
- For each person pᵢ: solo/rest time constraints and recurring holds
- For each person pᵢ: a set of free windows Fᵢ derived from their calendar(s)
- A scheduling horizon T (e.g., the next 7 days)

**Find:**
- A set of Time Blocks B that assigns specific time windows to specific pairs/groups

**Maximizing:**
- Maximin fairness: maximize the satisfaction of the least-satisfied person

**Subject to:**
- No person is double-booked
- All hard minimums (needs) are met, or the system reports that they cannot be met and explains why
- Solo/rest time constraints are respected
- Recurring holds are respected
- Each time block's duration matches its event type's requirements
- Locked blocks from reshuffles are treated as immovable

### 5.2 Solution Strategy

At polycule scale (typically 3–15 people, 2–20 relationships), this problem is small enough for exact solutions. The solver does not need AI/LLM — it's pure constraint optimization.

**Recommended approach: Mixed Integer Linear Program (MILP)**

1. **Discretize time** into 30-minute slots across the scheduling horizon
2. **Decision variables:** binary variables xᵢⱼₜ = 1 if persons i and j have a scheduled block starting at slot t
3. **Objective function:** maximize the minimum satisfaction ratio across all people, where satisfaction ratio = (actual hours achieved) / (preferred hours stated)
4. **Constraints:**
   - No overlap: for each person, at most one active block per time slot
   - Minimum hours (needs): sum of scheduled hours per relationship ≥ stated minimum
   - Solo time: unscheduled slots per period ≥ stated minimum
   - Event type minimums: count of each event type ≥ stated minimum per type
   - Availability: blocks can only occupy slots where ALL participants are free
   - Duration: blocks must be contiguous and match event type duration
   - Recurring holds: pre-assigned slots are fixed

**Solver:** Use an open-source MILP solver like [HiGHS](https://highs.dev/) (fast, MIT-licensed, has JavaScript and Python bindings) or OR-Tools from Google.

**Performance expectation:** For 10 people, 7-day horizon, 30-min slots (336 slots), this is ~3,000 binary variables. HiGHS solves problems of this scale in under a second.

### 5.3 Handling Infeasibility

When not everyone's needs can be met (which will happen), the solver should:

1. First attempt: satisfy all hard minimums. If feasible, proceed to optimize soft preferences.
2. If infeasible: relax hard minimums proportionally and report which needs couldn't be met and why (e.g., "You and Alex both need 10 hours/week together, but you only share 6 hours of free time this week").
3. Generate a **satisfaction report** per person showing: need fulfillment %, preference fulfillment %, breakdown by partner and event type.

### 5.4 Where AI Fits

The optimizer itself is deterministic and scriptable. AI (LLM) is used for:

- **Conversational preference setting:** "I want to see Alex about twice a week, mostly evenings" → parsed into structured preferences
- **Schedule explanation:** "Why didn't I get an overnight with Jordan this week?" → LLM reads solver output and explains in natural language
- **Reshuffle negotiation:** "Something came up Thursday, can we move things around?" → LLM interprets the request, calls the solver with updated constraints, and explains the proposed changes
- **Telegram bot interactions:** All natural language I/O through the Telegram bot

### 6.2 Technology Choices

| Layer | Technology | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|

| Database | PostgreSQL | Relational data (graph of people/relationships), ACID compliance for schedule locks |
| Cache/Sessions | Redis | Fast session management, pub/sub for real-time updates |
| Optimizer | Python microservice | Best ecosystem for MILP solvers (HiGHS, OR-Tools) |

| Telegram Bot | grammy (TypeScript) | Modern, well-maintained Telegram bot framework |

| Hosting | Self-hostable via Docker Compose; reference deployment on Railway/Fly.io | Easy self-hosting, low-cost managed deployment |
| AI/LLM | Anthropic Claude API (via API calls from backend) | Conversational preference parsing, schedule explanation, Telegram bot NLU |

### 6.3 Database Schema (Simplified)

```sql
-- People
CREATE TABLE persons (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  display_name TEXT NOT NULL,
  email TEXT UNIQUE NOT NULL,
  timezone TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'America/Denver',
  telegram_handle TEXT,
  solo_min_free_evenings_per_week INT DEFAULT 0,
  solo_min_free_weekend_days_per_month INT DEFAULT 0,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW()
);

-- Partner Relationships (bidirectional)
CREATE TABLE partnerships (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  person_a UUID REFERENCES persons(id),
  person_b UUID REFERENCES persons(id),
  status TEXT CHECK (status IN ('invited','active','paused','archived')),
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW(),
  UNIQUE (person_a, person_b)
);

-- Per-person preferences for a partnership
CREATE TABLE partnership_preferences (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  partnership_id UUID REFERENCES partnerships(id),
  person_id UUID REFERENCES persons(id),  -- whose preferences these are
  cadence TEXT CHECK (cadence IN ('weekly','biweekly','monthly')),
  need_min_hours NUMERIC DEFAULT 0,       -- hard minimum
  pref_ideal_hours NUMERIC DEFAULT 0,     -- soft target
  need_min_date_nights INT DEFAULT 0,
  pref_date_nights INT DEFAULT 0,
  pref_overnights INT DEFAULT 0,
  pref_daytime_hangs INT DEFAULT 0,
  recurring_holds JSONB DEFAULT '[]',     -- e.g. [{"day":"tuesday","type":"date_night"}]
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW()
);

-- Pods
CREATE TABLE pods (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  name TEXT NOT NULL,
  scheduling_cadence TEXT DEFAULT 'weekly',
  planning_horizon_weeks INT DEFAULT 1,
  review_window_hours INT DEFAULT 48,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW()
);

CREATE TABLE pod_members (
  pod_id UUID REFERENCES pods(id),
  person_id UUID REFERENCES persons(id),
  role TEXT DEFAULT 'member',
  joined_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW(),
  PRIMARY KEY (pod_id, person_id)
);

-- Pod-level scheduling preferences
CREATE TABLE pod_preferences (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  pod_id UUID REFERENCES pods(id),
  pref_full_pod_gatherings_per_month INT DEFAULT 0,
  pref_full_pod_gathering_hours NUMERIC DEFAULT 3,
  subgroup_configs JSONB DEFAULT '[]',
  -- e.g. [{"members":["uuid1","uuid2"],"frequency":"monthly","hours":2}]
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW()
);

-- Generated schedule blocks
CREATE TABLE time_blocks (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  cycle_id UUID NOT NULL,               -- which scheduling cycle generated this
  event_type TEXT NOT NULL,
  start_time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  end_time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  status TEXT CHECK (status IN ('proposed','accepted','locked','declined','reshuffled')),
  source_pod_id UUID REFERENCES pods(id),
  satisfaction_notes JSONB,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW()
);

CREATE TABLE time_block_participants (
  time_block_id UUID REFERENCES time_blocks(id),
  person_id UUID REFERENCES persons(id),
  response TEXT CHECK (response IN ('pending','accepted','declined')),
  PRIMARY KEY (time_block_id, person_id)
);

-- Calendar integration tokens (encrypted)
CREATE TABLE calendar_connections (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  person_id UUID REFERENCES persons(id),
  provider TEXT CHECK (provider IN ('google','icloud','outlook')),
  encrypted_tokens BYTEA NOT NULL,
  scopes TEXT[],
  last_synced_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW()
);

-- Scheduling cycles
CREATE TABLE scheduling_cycles (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY,
  triggered_by UUID REFERENCES persons(id),  -- or system
  horizon_start TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  horizon_end TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  status TEXT CHECK (status IN ('collecting','optimizing','proposed','negotiating','locked')),
  satisfaction_report JSONB,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW()
);
```

## 7. Frontend Design Direction

### 7.1 Design Principles

- **Warm, not clinical.** Soft colors, rounded shapes, gentle animations. This is about love, not logistics.
- **Calendar-forward.** The primary view is a beautiful weekly/monthly calendar showing your time blocks, color-coded by partner/pod.
- **Glanceable satisfaction.** Small, non-judgmental indicators showing how close you are to your ideal time distribution. Think gentle progress rings, not red warning badges.
- **Mobile-first.** The PWA should feel native on a phone. Thumb-friendly navigation, swipe gestures for accepting/declining proposals.

### 7.2 Key Screens

1. **Home / Calendar View** — Your week at a glance. Time blocks color-coded by partner. Tap a block to see details or propose a change.
2. **Partners** — Your partners listed with relationship health indicators (satisfaction %, upcoming time together). Tap to adjust preferences.
3. **Pods** — Your pods with member lists and pod-level schedule. Pod gathering proposals.
4. **Schedule Review** — When a new proposal arrives: side-by-side current vs. proposed, satisfaction scores, accept/negotiate/decline flow.
5. **Settings** — Calendar connections, notification preferences, solo time rules, Telegram connection.

### 7.3 Color & Visual Language

- Palette: warm earth tones, soft gradients (terracotta, sage, warm gold, dusty rose)
- Each partner gets an assigned color (user-customizable) that persists across all views
- Pod gatherings use a blended/gradient of member colors
- Typography: friendly rounded sans-serif (e.g., Nunito, Quicksand)
- Iconography: soft, hand-drawn style icons — not geometric corporate glyphs

## 9. Open Questions & Future Considerations

### Deferred to v2

- **Shared expenses / date planning:** Integration with splitting costs, restaurant suggestions, activity planning
- **Relationship check-ins:** Periodic prompts for partners to reflect on relationship satisfaction (beyond just scheduling)
- **NFC / proximity awareness:** "You and Alex are both free right now and nearby" — spontaneous quality time suggestions
- **Monogamous fork:** Simplified version for couples who want to prioritize date nights (could be a "lite mode" rather than a separate product)
- **Group calendar export:** ICS feed per pod for people who want to see pod schedules in their native calendar app
- **Conflict early warning:** "Your needs with Alex and Jordan add up to more hours than you typically have free — consider adjusting"

### Architectural Decisions Still Needed

- **License choice:** AGPL (strong copyleft, ensures self-hosted modifications stay open) vs. MIT (maximum adoption) vs. BSL (source-available with delayed open source)
- **LLM dependency:** Should the core product work without AI entirely (AI as enhancement only), or is AI integral to the experience? Recommendation: core optimizer and flows work without AI; AI enhances Telegram bot and conversational UX.
- **Calendar write-back scope:** Should Pod Life create calendar events, or just propose times and let people add them manually? Recommendation: auto-create with user's explicit opt-in per calendar.
- **Multi-timezone pods:** Partners in different timezones add complexity to "evening" and "overnight" definitions. The solver handles this naturally (all times in UTC), but the UX needs to display times in each person's local timezone.
