## The Heart of It

You love more than one person.

That's it. That's where everything starts. Not with calendars, not with algorithms, not with fairness constraints. It starts with the simple, beautiful, sometimes overwhelming reality that your heart holds more than one person — and you want to show up for all of them.

Love means care. Care means quality time. And quality time means actually being present with the people who matter to you — not squeezing them into the margins of an overbooked life.

Pod Life exists because the hardest part of loving multiple people isn't the loving. It's the logistics. It's the group texts about "when are you free?" that never converge. It's the guilt of realizing you haven't had a real date night with someone in three weeks — not because you don't care, but because the weeks just kept filling up.

We built Pod Life to take the logistical weight off your shoulders so you can focus on what actually matters: being together.

## Tagline

> **I want what you want.**

This is the animating principle of the app and of the relationships it serves. It means:

- I genuinely want my partners to have the time and connection they need — with me and with each other.
- I trust that they want the same for me.
- When everyone is on the same team, we can build a schedule that actually honors what each person needs.
- The system isn't about compromise or sacrifice. It's about finding the arrangement where everyone gets more of what they want.

"I want what you want" isn't naive. It's the most practical thing a polycule can believe, because when it's true, coordination becomes possible. Pod Life is the tool that makes that coordination effortless.

## Brand Positioning

### What Pod Life Is

A relationship-time companion for people who love more than one person. It helps you and your partners find time for what matters — date nights, overnights, lazy mornings, pod gatherings — by understanding what everyone needs and gently organizing your shared time around it.

### What Pod Life Is Not

- Not a dating app.
- Not a "relationship management platform" (we manage time, not relationships).
- Not a corporate scheduling tool with a heart emoji bolted on.
- Not prescriptive about what polyamory should look like.

### Who Pod Life Is For

Every kind of polyamorous arrangement. Triads, quads, Vs, Ws, kitchen-table polycules, parallel poly, solo poly, relationship anarchists, and configurations that don't have names yet. Pod Life doesn't know what shape your love is. It just knows you want more of it.

## Voice Principles

These five principles guide every piece of text in the app — from the landing page to a push notification to an error message.

### 1. Lead with Love, Not Logistics

The app is a scheduling tool. The brand is not. We never lead with the mechanics. We lead with why the mechanics matter: because you love these people and you want to be present for them.

**Yes:**
"Let's find time for the people you love."

**No:**
"Optimize your polyamorous schedule with AI-powered constraint satisfaction."

**Yes:**
"Tell us about your relationships — what does quality time look like with each person?"

**No:**
"Configure scheduling parameters for each partner relationship."

### 2. Honor the Uniqueness

No two polyamorous arrangements look alike. The app celebrates this rather than flattening it. We never assume a default relationship structure. We never use language that implies hierarchy unless the user has explicitly chosen it. We always make space for "none of the above."

**Yes:**
"Every relationship has its own rhythm. Some partners want long weekends together. Some want Tuesday-night rituals. Some want spontaneous afternoons. Tell us what feels right for each one."

**No:**
"Set your primary and secondary partner preferences."

**Yes:**
"Your pod, your way. Name it, shape it, invite whoever belongs."

**No:**
"Create a group and add members."

### 3. Sovereignty and Connection

These two values live together. Pod Life protects your autonomy (your time, your boundaries, your choices) while also honoring your interdependence (you share your life with people who also have needs and boundaries). The voice holds both without collapsing into either.

Sovereignty means:
- You're always in choice. The app proposes, you decide.
- Your alone time is sacred. It's not "unscheduled" — it's protected.
- Your preferences are yours. They're private unless you choose to share them.

Connection means:
- You're not scheduling in a vacuum. Your partners' needs are real and visible.
- The app helps you see the whole picture, not just your corner of it.
- When something shifts, it ripples with care, not chaos.

**Yes:**
"You've asked for two free evenings a week for yourself. We'll always protect that."

**No:**
"Solo time constraint configured: 2 evenings/week blocked."

**Yes:**
"Alex would love a date night this week, and Thursday evening works for both of you."

**No:**
"Unallocated time block detected for Partner A. Scheduling opportunity identified."

### 4. Gentle, Not Precious

The voice is warm but not saccharine. It's grounded. Polyamorous people deal with real complexity — jealousy, time scarcity, hard conversations, metamour dynamics — and the app shouldn't pretend otherwise. But it also shouldn't dwell there. The tone is: "Yeah, this stuff is hard sometimes. Here's a tool that makes the logistical part easier, so you have more energy for the emotional part."

We can be playful. We can be direct. We never condescend, and we never oversimplify.

**Yes:**
"This week's a tight one. You and Jordan only have one overlapping evening. Want to make it count?"

**No:**
"Unfortunately, insufficient mutual availability was detected for this pairing. 😢"

**Yes:**
"Heads up — your date nights with Sam and your overnights with Alex add up to more time than you've got free. Something's gotta give. Want to adjust?"

**No:**
"WARNING: Infeasible constraint configuration detected. Total requested hours exceed available capacity by 37%."

### 5. The Math Is There When You Want It

Pod Life does sophisticated optimization under the hood. The brand doesn't hide this — it's part of the credibility. But the math is always in service of the feeling, never the other way around. Lead with what the user gets (a schedule where everyone's cared for), and let them dig into how it works if they're curious.

**Yes:**
"Pod Life looks at everyone's calendars, listens to what each person needs, and finds a schedule that works for the whole pod. The goal is simple: get everyone as close to their ideal week as possible."

**No:**
"Our maximin fairness algorithm solves a mixed-integer linear program to optimize the minimum satisfaction ratio across all relationship dyads."

When explaining the optimizer to curious users (in settings, help docs, or onboarding):

**Yes:**
"Here's how it works: each person says what they need and what they'd love. Pod Life finds the schedule where the person getting the least of what they want is getting as much as possible. It's not about averaging — it's about making sure nobody gets left behind."

**No:**
"The solver maximizes z subject to z ≤ satisfaction_ratio(p) for all p ∈ P."

## Vocabulary Guide

Words shape how people feel about a tool. These vocabulary choices encode the brand values.

### Words We Use

| Concept | Pod Life Term | Why |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| The people you're in relationships with | **Partners** | Warm, egalitarian, universally understood |
| A named group of connected people | **Pod** | Cozy, organic, suggests a safe container |
| What someone requires at minimum | **Needs** | Direct, honest, emotionally grounded |
| What someone would love to have | **Hopes** | Warmer than "preferences" — these are wishes, not parameters |
| A proposed schedule | **Suggestion** | The app suggests, it doesn't dictate |
| A confirmed schedule | **Plan** | Simple, human, collaborative |
| Rearranging something | **Reshuffling** | Light, undramatic, things-happen energy |
| How close you are to your ideal | **Fulfillment** | Positive frame — how much you're getting, not how much you're missing |
| Time for yourself | **Rest** or **Solo time** | Dignified, not a leftover category |
| The system's scheduling run | **Check-in** | The app "checks in" with everyone's needs — not "runs a cycle" |

### Words We Avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use Instead |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| **Optimize / Optimization** | Clinical, transactional | "Find time," "make space," "work out a plan" |
| **Allocate / Allocation** | Resource-management language | "Set aside," "make time for," "save time for" |
| **Constraint** | Engineering jargon | "Boundary," "need," "what works for you" |
| **Configure / Configuration** | Enterprise software | "Set up," "customize," "tell us about" |
| **Stakeholder / User** | Corporate | "You," "your partner," by name when possible |
| **Primary / Secondary** | Hierarchical — not everyone uses this | Only if the user explicitly chooses this language |
| **Schedule** (as a verb, for what the app does) | Implies the app decides | "Suggest," "propose," "find time" |
| **Scarce / Scarcity** | Anxiety-producing, deficit framing | "Busy weeks," "tight," "a lot going on" |
| **Fair / Fairness** (as the lead concept) | Implies potential unfairness, sounds legalistic | "Balanced," "everyone gets cared for," "works for the whole pod" |
| **Demand / Requirement** | Harsh, transactional | "Need," "hope," "what matters to you" |
| **Failed / Error / Warning** | Alarming | "Heads up," "couldn't quite," "here's what happened" |

### Special Case: "Fair" and "Fairness"

These words aren't banned — they're repositioned. Fairness is an outcome of the system, not its purpose. The purpose is care. Fairness is the mechanism that ensures care reaches everyone.

**Don't say:** "Pod Life creates fair schedules."
**Do say:** "Pod Life finds a plan where everyone's cared for."

**Don't say:** "The algorithm ensures fairness across all relationships."
**Do say:** "The system makes sure nobody gets left behind."

Fairness language is appropriate in explanatory/technical contexts (help docs, the "how it works" section, developer documentation). It's not appropriate in emotional/motivational contexts (onboarding, notifications, the core UI).

## Tone by Context

The voice adapts to context while staying true to the principles.

### Onboarding

**Tone:** Warm welcome, gentle curiosity, zero judgment.

The person is trusting us with deeply personal information about their love life. Meet that trust with openness and care.

```
Welcome to Pod Life 🏠

You're here because you love more than one person and you want 
to show up for all of them.

We're going to help you do that.

First, tell us a little about your relationships — not the 
details, just the shape. Who are the people you want to make 
time for? What does quality time look like with each of them?

There are no wrong answers. Every relationship is different, 
and Pod Life is designed to meet yours exactly where it is.
```

```
Let's talk about what you need.

For each partner, you can tell us two things:

  What you need — the minimum quality time that keeps 
  your relationship feeling healthy and connected. 
  We'll always protect this.

  What you hope for — your ideal week. The version 
  where everything lines up perfectly. We'll get you 
  as close to this as we can.

Don't overthink it. You can always adjust later as 
you learn what works.
```

### Pod Creation

**Tone:** Celebratory, community-oriented, playful.

```
Let's make your pod.

A pod is just a name for the people you share your life with. 
It could be your triad, your polycule, your nesting partners, 
your chosen family — whatever feels right.

Give it a name. Something that feels like home.
```

### Weekly Suggestion (Notification)

**Tone:** Brief, warm, actionable.

```
Your week is coming together ✨

Pod Life found time for a date night with Alex on Thursday 
and brunch with the whole pod on Sunday. 

Take a look and let us know if it feels right.
```

### Satisfaction Report

**Tone:** Honest, encouraging, never guilt-inducing.

Frame fulfillment positively. The glass is described by how full it is.

```
Here's how your week is shaping up:

Alex — 💛💛💛💛💛💛💛💛○○ — 80% of what you hoped for
Jordan — 💛💛💛💛💛💛○○○○ — 60% of what you hoped for

You and Jordan only had two overlapping free evenings this 
week — that's what made it tight. Next week looks more open.
```

**Never say:** "You're only giving Jordan 60% of what they need."
**Never say:** "WARNING: Jordan's satisfaction is below threshold."

### When Needs Can't Be Met

**Tone:** Compassionate honesty. This is a moment where the app is surfacing a real tension in someone's life. Handle it with care.

```
Heads up — this week is really full.

You've said you need at least 6 hours with Alex and 
6 hours with Jordan, but you and Jordan only share 
about 4 free hours this week.

This isn't a problem with the app — it's just a 
busy week. A few options:

  • See if Jordan can shift something to open up 
    another evening
  • Adjust your hopes for this week and aim for 
    more next week  
  • Talk it through — sometimes knowing the constraint 
    helps the conversation

Whatever you decide, Pod Life will work with it.
```

### Reshuffle Request

**Tone:** Easy, undramatic, things-happen energy.

```
No worries — life happens.

If Thursday doesn't work anymore, Pod Life can try to 
find another time that works for both you and Alex. 
Your other plans stay put.

What changed? (This helps us find a better time.)
```

### Error / Failure States

**Tone:** Calm, clear, helpful. Never alarming. Never blame the user.

```
Hmm, we couldn't connect to your Google Calendar just now. 
This usually sorts itself out — we'll try again in a bit.

If it keeps happening, you might need to reconnect your 
calendar in Settings.
```

```
Something went wrong on our end while putting together 
this week's suggestion. We're on it.

Your current plan is still in place — nothing's changed.
```

### Telegram Bot

**Tone:** Friendly assistant. Slightly more casual than the app UI. Uses emoji naturally but not excessively.

```
Hey! 📅 Your week's suggestion is ready.

Thursday evening — date night with Alex
Saturday morning — brunch with the pod
Sunday afternoon — daytime hang with Jordan

Look good? Tap ✅ to lock it in, or open the app to tweak.
```

```
Alex requested a change to Thursday's date night — 
something came up at work.

Pod Life found Friday evening as an alternative. 
It works for both of you. Switch to Friday?
```

## Design Implications

The brand voice has direct implications for the visual design. These should be consistent:

### The Calendar Doesn't Feel Like a Calendar

The primary view should feel more like a week of plans than a corporate schedule grid. Time blocks should look like warm, rounded cards — not rigid rectangles. They should feel like something you're looking forward to, not something you're obligated to attend.

### Fulfillment Indicators Are Gentle

The satisfaction rings/bars should feel encouraging, not judgmental. Use warm fills (not progress-bar green), and frame the number positively. An 80% fulfillment ring should feel like "that's great" not "you're missing 20%."

Never use red for low fulfillment. Use softer warm tones that suggest "there's room for more" rather than "this is a problem."

### Names, Not Labels

Wherever possible, use people's chosen display names, not generic labels. "Date night with Alex" not "Date Night — Partner 1." "Your pod The Nest" not "Pod #1."

### Consent Is Visible

Every action the app takes on behalf of the user should be visible and reversible. Suggestions are clearly marked as suggestions. Locked plans are clearly marked as confirmed. The user always knows what the app has done and can always undo it.

## The Deeper Story (For Long-Form Content)

When writing landing page copy, blog posts, or about pages, this is the fuller narrative to draw from:

Polyamorous people share a beautiful and specific challenge. The love isn't the hard part. The logistics are. Every polyamorous person knows the dance: the shared Google Calendars, the group chats that never quite converge, the guilt of realizing you haven't had a real date night with someone in weeks — not because you don't care, but because life just keeps filling up.

The joke in every polyamorous community is that poly people spend more time scheduling than actually being together. Like most jokes, it's funny because it's painfully true.

Pod Life was built from inside this experience. Not as a techno-optimist fantasy about "optimizing relationships" — but as a genuinely caring tool for people who love more than one person and want to show up for all of them. The premise is simple: tell us who you love, tell us what quality time looks like for each relationship, and let us find a way to make it work.

Under the hood, Pod Life does something elegant. It looks at everyone's calendars, listens to what each person needs and hopes for, and finds a plan where the person getting the least of what they want is getting as much as possible. Not averaging. Not compromising. Finding the arrangement where everyone can flourish.

But the math isn't the point. The point is Sunday morning with the whole pod making pancakes. The point is Tuesday-night dates that actually happen instead of getting rescheduled into oblivion. The point is having enough time for yourself to be the kind of partner you want to be.

Pod Life is built for every configuration of polyamorous relationship — triads, quads, Vs, kitchen-table polycules, parallel poly, solo poly, relationship anarchists, and structures that don't have names yet. Because the one thing all polyamorous people have in common is that no two arrangements look alike. And any tool that serves this community has to be as flexible and unique as the relationships themselves.

I want what you want. That's the principle. When everyone genuinely wants their partners to have what they need, coordination becomes possible. Pod Life is what happens when that principle meets a really good scheduler.

## Voice Checklist

Before shipping any user-facing text, run it through these questions:

1. **Does it lead with care, not mechanics?** If the first thing someone reads is a technical concept, rewrite.

2. **Would this feel warm if you read it to a partner?** If it sounds like a software manual, rewrite.

3. **Does it assume a specific relationship structure?** If it implies hierarchy, exclusivity, or a "default" way of doing poly, rewrite.

4. **Does it respect autonomy?** Does the language make clear that the user is in choice? That the app suggests but doesn't decide?

5. **Is it honest about limitations?** When something can't be done, does the text say so directly and compassionately, without blame?

6. **Does it name people, not roles?** Wherever possible, use display names instead of "Partner A" or "your partner."

7. **Is the framing positive?** Is fulfillment described by what you're getting, not what you're missing? Is a busy week framed as "a lot going on" rather than "a scarcity of time"?

8. **Could a relationship anarchist use this without flinching?** If the language assumes hierarchy, nesting, or any specific poly structure, it needs to be more open.
